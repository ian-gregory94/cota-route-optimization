## How to tell, for any artifact

Open its experiment.json and read `evaluator`. `common_lines: same_route` with `source: explicit` is Model B, declared. `null` means the run predates provenance recording and the model must be established another way — for Experiment 2 that means checking whether the cell key carries |same_route|.

## Experiment 3 — Phase A1's first 10 states, superseded 2026-08-30

`outputs/exp3/superseded/stageA_*` — ten singles and three zero-edit replicates
scored before three defects in the contract validator were found and fixed.

**The scores themselves are unaffected** — the validator refuses states, it does
not influence scoring — but each row carries a `contract.facts` block computed
under the superseded rules: `veh_hours` holding the edited baseline's hours
rather than the optimized plan's, and `network_edit_distance_pct` computed by
matching route ids rather than stop-visits. Keeping rows whose provenance block
means something different from every other row's is precisely the failure this
manifest exists to prevent, and ten states is forty minutes to redo.

The defects, found when a shard refused 24 mutations in one second:

* the envelope was checked on the **edited baseline** rather than the optimized
  plan, refusing 42 of 84 pool mutations for exceeding budget by fractions of a
  percent — backwards, since the method is mutate then re-optimize frequency
  inside the envelope;
* any state leaving a stop **served by no route** was refused, which is what a
  truncation does and truncation is permitted under a 40% cap — the contract
  forbids changing which stops *exist*, and sole access is protected separately;
* **edit distance matched route ids**, so a splice read as deleting two routes
  and creating a third. Every splice scored 18–22% against a 15% line, making
  the contract contradict itself since it lists splice as permitted and quotas
  twelve.

Together they refused **60 of 84** mutations across every kind. Experiment 3
would have evaluated the 24 cheapest and reported the validator's bugs as a
finding about COTA's network.

`outputs/exp3/validator_invariant.json` is the check that now stands against
this: the validator must accept all twelve candidates Experiment 2B applied,
scored and reported. It does, and the pool passes 84/84.
